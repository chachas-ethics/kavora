The Lake Nakuru experience

Where the landscape
comes alive.

Set within Kenya's Great Rift Valley, Lake Nakuru brings together a shallow alkaline lake, open grassland, rocky escarpments and pockets of acacia woodland. Its changing habitats support a remarkable variety of birdlife and mammals.

The park is especially known for its rhino conservation, birdwatching and scenic viewpoints. From quiet woodland drives to views across the lake, the experience rewards travellers who appreciate diversity within a compact landscape.

A smaller wilderness, rich in detail.

02 / Timing your journey

When to
go.

Lake Nakuru can be visited throughout the year. Conditions influence the scenery, birdlife and driving experience, so the right timing depends on what you hope to see.

Wildlife sightings and flamingo numbers are not guaranteed. Lake conditions and rainfall patterns can change from season to season.

January — March

Warm, often drier days

A useful period to consider for game drives and wildlife viewing. Early departures help make the most of cooler temperatures and softer light.

April — May

The green season

Rain can bring greener scenery and dramatic skies. Some tracks may become muddy, and travel plans should allow for changing road conditions.

June — October

A popular safari period

The drier months are often favoured for exploring the park. Wildlife viewing conditions can be rewarding, though birdlife and lake levels still vary.

November — December

Fresh colour returns

Short rains can refresh the landscape and create attractive photographic conditions. Exact rainfall timing varies from year to year.

Before
you go.

Lake Nakuru National Park is commonly reached by road from Nairobi. The journey duration depends on traffic, road conditions and your starting point. A private transfer can be arranged as part of a wider Kenya itinerary.

One or two nights can provide a useful introduction to the park. Additional time may suit travellers with a particular interest in birdlife, photography or combining the lake with other destinations in the Rift Valley.

Flamingos may be present, but their numbers and distribution change with lake conditions and food availability. They should not be treated as a guaranteed sighting. The park offers other birdlife and wildlife experiences as well.

Pack comfortable neutral-coloured clothing, a warm layer for early mornings, sun protection, a hat, binoculars and a camera if desired. A light waterproof layer can be useful during rainy periods.

The lake, birdlife, rhinos, woodland and elevated viewpoints offer a range of photographic subjects. Early and late light can be rewarding. Wildlife encounters and lake conditions vary, so flexibility is useful.
