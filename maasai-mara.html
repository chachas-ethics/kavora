02 / The destination

Where the wild moves freely, and every horizon holds another story.

In south-western Kenya, the Maasai Mara forms part of the greater Mara–Serengeti ecosystem, a landscape of open grasslands, winding rivers and wildlife that follows the changing seasons.

Lions rest beneath acacias, cheetahs scan the plains and enormous herds of wildebeest and zebra move across the grasslands during the migration. Beyond the celebrated spectacles, the Mara rewards travellers who make time for quieter moments and thoughtful guiding.

04 / Planning your visit

When to
go.

The Mara is rewarding throughout the year, but seasonal rainfall changes the landscape, wildlife movement and overall safari experience. Your ideal dates depend on what you most want to see.

A useful consideration

The Great Migration follows rainfall and fresh grazing, not a fixed timetable. A river crossing is never guaranteed on a particular date.

July – October

The migration season

Peak demand

This is the best-known period for migration herds and possible Mara River crossings. Demand for sought-after camps is often high, and the timing of wildlife activity varies with conditions.

January – March

Big cats & open plains

Resident wildlife

A strong option for general wildlife viewing and predator encounters. The landscape can be drier and the experience may be quieter than during peak migration periods.

November – December

The green season

Fresh landscapes

The short rains can bring fresh grass, dramatic skies and a renewed landscape. Wildlife remains present year-round, while rainfall and herd movements vary.

April – May

The long rains

Lush & changeable

Rainfall can create striking green scenery and a different atmosphere. Some tracks may become difficult, and individual camps may change their operating schedules. Confirm conditions before travelling.

No. The Great Migration is a natural movement influenced by rainfall, grazing and water availability. Herds may gather near a river for a long time before crossing, and a crossing may not happen during your stay. Plan for the wider Mara experience rather than a guaranteed event.

The Mara is known for lions, cheetahs, leopards, elephants, buffalo, giraffes, zebra, wildebeest and numerous antelope species. Hippos, crocodiles and a rich variety of birds can also be seen. Wildlife sightings are never guaranteed.

Three days and two nights is a useful starting point. Four days and three nights gives you more time for game drives, photography and a less rushed experience. Longer stays can be worthwhile if wildlife observation is a central priority.

Travellers commonly reach the Mara by road from Nairobi or by domestic flight to a suitable airstrip, followed by a transfer to their camp or lodge. The best option depends on your itinerary, budget, dates and accommodation location.

Pack comfortable neutral-coloured clothing, a warm layer for early mornings, sun protection, a hat, comfortable shoes and binoculars if available. Rain protection can be useful in wetter months. Check any luggage restrictions if your itinerary includes a light aircraft flight.
