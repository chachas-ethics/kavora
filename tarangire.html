A Different Kind of Safari

Where the wild feels timeless.

Tarangire National Park offers a distinctive expression of Tanzania's wilderness. Its open savannah, towering baobabs, river valleys, and seasonal marshes create a landscape that changes beautifully with the seasons.

During the dry season, the Tarangire River becomes a vital source of water, drawing elephants and other wildlife into the surrounding landscape. The result is an extraordinary setting for observing animal behaviour and experiencing the quieter side of a northern Tanzania safari.

Planning Your Visit

The right
time to go.

Tarangire changes with the seasons. The best time for your safari depends on the wildlife encounters you hope to experience, your preferred scenery, and how you plan to combine the park with other destinations.

We can help you select travel dates and arrange a route that fits your priorities.

Discuss Your Travel Dates

June – October

Dry Season

Often a particularly rewarding period for wildlife viewing. As the landscape dries, animals may concentrate around remaining water sources, including the Tarangire River.

November – December

Short Rains

The landscape can become greener as seasonal rains arrive. Wildlife distribution and road conditions may change, giving the park a different atmosphere from the dry months.

January – March

Green Landscapes

Depending on rainfall, the park can offer greener scenery, fresh vegetation, and a different photographic experience. Wildlife is generally more dispersed than during peak dry-season concentrations.

April – May

Long Rains

Rainfall can be heavier, vegetation becomes lush, and travel conditions may be more challenging. Check current conditions and accommodation availability before booking.

Seasonal patterns vary from year to year. These are general planning guidelines, not guarantees of weather or wildlife sightings.

FAQs.

A few useful answers to help you prepare for your Tarangire National Park safari.

Ask Us a Question →
What is Tarangire National Park known for? +
Tarangire is particularly known for its elephant herds, ancient baobab trees, varied habitats, and seasonal wildlife concentrations around the Tarangire River.
When is the best time to visit Tarangire? +
The dry season, generally from June to October, is often favoured for wildlife viewing because animals may gather around remaining water sources. Other months can offer greener scenery and a different experience. Conditions vary each year, so your preferred travel dates and interests should guide the decision.
How many days should I spend in Tarangire? +
One or two full days can work well for many itineraries. The right duration depends on your interests, travel pace, accommodation location, and whether you are combining Tarangire with other northern Tanzania parks.
Can Tarangire be combined with Serengeti and Ngorongoro? +
Yes. Tarangire can be included in a northern Tanzania circuit with destinations such as Serengeti, Ngorongoro Crater, and Lake Manyara. The best route depends on your trip length, entry and exit points, and the experiences you want to prioritise.
What wildlife might I see in Tarangire? +
Depending on the season and location, visitors may encounter elephants, giraffes, zebras, wildebeest, buffalo, antelope, and a variety of bird species. Predators are also present, but sightings are never guaranteed.
Is Tarangire suitable for a private safari? +
Yes. A private safari can offer flexibility in daily scheduling, wildlife viewing, and accommodation choices. Your itinerary can be designed around your group size, interests, budget, and preferred travel style.
How do I plan a Tarangire safari with Kavora Africa? +
Contact Kavora Africa with your intended travel dates, number of travellers, approximate trip length, and interests. You can then discuss an itinerary that includes Tarangire and any additional destinations you would like to explore.
