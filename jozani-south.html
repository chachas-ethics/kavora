A different side of the island

Where the island
slows down.

Beyond Zanzibar's historic streets and northern beaches lies a quieter landscape shaped by tropical forest, traditional villages and a coastline that feels wonderfully unhurried.

The south invites you to experience the island at a gentler pace. Walk beneath the canopy of Jozani Forest, encounter the wildlife that makes this ecosystem unique, discover coastal traditions, and make time for the simple pleasure of being here.

At Kavora, we bring these moments together into considered journeys, balancing nature, local culture and restorative time by the ocean.

01 / Nature

The green heart of Zanzibar

Jozani
Forest.

Step into one of Zanzibar's most remarkable natural environments. Jozani is known for its rich forest habitat, distinctive mangrove ecosystems and the endemic Zanzibar red colobus monkey.

Guided walks offer a closer look at the island's biodiversity, from towering trees and forest plants to the shifting landscapes where woodland gives way to mangrove.

Known for

Red colobus monkeys · Forest walks · Mangrove boardwalks · Birdlife

02 / Coast

The southern shoreline

Kizimkazi
by the sea.

On Zanzibar's southern coast, Kizimkazi offers a glimpse of a long-standing relationship between island communities and the sea. Traditional fishing culture, coastal scenery and open horizons give this part of the island a character of its own.

Boat excursions are available in the area, including trips where dolphins may be encountered in their natural environment. Sightings are never guaranteed, and responsible operators should prioritise distance, animal welfare and respectful observation.

Known for

Coastal villages · Traditional boats · Ocean excursions · Seascapes

03 / Island life

Traditions of the south

Makunduchi
and beyond.

Discover a more everyday expression of Zanzibar in the island's southern villages. Here, the pace of life is shaped by community, the seasons, fishing, farming and the traditions carried between generations.

With thoughtful local guidance, village visits can offer meaningful encounters with Swahili culture, local food and island crafts. Experiences should be arranged with respect for residents and their privacy, with community consent at the centre.

Known for

Swahili heritage · Village life · Local food · Cultural encounters

A few useful details

Before you
set off.

A little preparation helps make your time in southern Zanzibar more comfortable, considered and rewarding.

Zanzibar can be visited throughout much of the year. The drier periods, generally June to October and January to February, are popular for outdoor activities. Rainfall, humidity and sea conditions vary, so we recommend planning around your preferred experiences and checking conditions closer to departure.

Many visitors allow around two to three hours for a guided forest visit, including time for the main trails and mangrove area when accessible. Your final schedule will depend on your guide, route, weather and onward plans.

Jozani is one of the best-known places to learn about and observe the endemic Zanzibar red colobus monkey. They are wild animals, however, so sightings and viewing conditions cannot be guaranteed. Visitors should follow guide instructions and avoid feeding or disturbing wildlife.
