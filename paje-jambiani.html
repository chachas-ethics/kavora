The South-Eastern Shore

Life follows
the rhythm of the tide.

Along Zanzibar's south-eastern coast, Paje and Jambiani offer an island experience shaped by changing tides, open horizons and a deep connection to the sea.

Paje is known for its kitesurfing culture, lively beach atmosphere and long stretches of white sand. Further along the coast, Jambiani offers a more village-centred rhythm, where fishing traditions, seaweed farming and everyday coastal life remain part of the landscape.

Together, they create a destination for travellers who want more than a beach holiday: a chance to move between adventure and stillness, local culture and private moments, all at their own pace.

Two Shores, Different Rhythms

The energy
of Paje.

Paje brings together an expansive shoreline, a relaxed international beach scene and ideal conditions for wind-powered water sports during suitable seasons. Kitesurfing is part of the area's identity, while beach cafés, ocean excursions and open-air evenings give the village its distinctive energy.

Spend your days learning a new skill on the water, enjoying the beach or discovering the quieter stretches of coast beyond the main village.

Before You Arrive

A few things
worth knowing.

A little preparation leaves more room to enjoy the island. Here are a few things to consider when planning a stay on Zanzibar's south-eastern coast.

June to October generally offers drier weather, while January and February are also popular for beach holidays. The south-eastern coast is strongly influenced by tides, and kitesurfing conditions vary with the season. Plan around the activities that matter most to you.

Paje is particularly associated with kitesurfing, beach cafés and a lively international atmosphere. Jambiani has a more village-centred character and a strong connection to fishing and seaweed farming. Both can be combined in one trip.

Yes. Tides significantly change the appearance and accessibility of the south-eastern shoreline. At low tide, the water can retreat far from the beach, exposing tidal flats and seaweed areas. Swimming and water activities should be planned around local conditions.

Beginner lessons may be available through local kite schools. Conditions, wind and instructor availability determine the right time to learn. Lessons should be booked with qualified instructors and appropriate safety equipment.

Yes. Many itineraries combine time on the south-eastern beaches with Stone Town's historic architecture, markets and Swahili heritage. Allow for road travel between locations when planning your schedule.
