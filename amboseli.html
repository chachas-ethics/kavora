02 / The destination

A landscape defined by space, presence and the quiet movement of the wild.

At the foot of Mount Kilimanjaro, Amboseli unfolds across open grasslands, acacia-dotted plains and seasonal wetlands. Its changing light and expansive views give the landscape a distinctive sense of scale.

The park is especially known for its elephants, often seen moving across the plains in family groups. With a considered pace and thoughtful guiding, a day here can become less about covering distance and more about observing the details of the natural world.

04 / Planning your visit

When to
go.

Amboseli changes with rainfall, water availability and the movement of wildlife. The right time to visit depends on the balance you want between wildlife viewing, atmosphere and landscape.

A useful consideration

Kilimanjaro views depend on weather and cloud cover. No month guarantees a clear mountain, so allow time and keep expectations flexible.

June – October

The drier months

Often favourable for viewing

Drier conditions can make wildlife easier to observe around reliable water sources. Days are often sunny, although mornings can be cool and mountain visibility varies.

November – December

The short rains

Seasonal contrast

Short rains may bring fresh growth, changing skies and a greener appearance. Rainfall varies from year to year, and road conditions can change.

January – February

A warmer interval

Variable conditions

Often warmer, with conditions influenced by the preceding rains and local water availability. This can be a good period for travellers seeking a quieter pace outside peak dates.

March – May

The long rains

Lush and changeable

Rain can transform the landscape, but heavier showers may affect access and travel times. Confirm lodge operations and road conditions before travelling.
